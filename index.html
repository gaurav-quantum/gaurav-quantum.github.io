A longer-term thread

From mathematical structure to experimental usefulness

My work often starts with a structural question in quantum information and ends by asking what that structure means for an actual circuit, algorithm, or device. That bridge — from abstract theory to experimentally meaningful diagnostics — is a recurring theme across my research.

What I'm thinking about now

From mitigation
to correction.

After several years working on quantum error mitigation, I am shifting more of my attention toward the structural power and limitations of quantum error correction.

At the same time, I continue to explore quantum resources and magic, especially the connection between resource theories, statistical mechanics, and computational advantage.

About

Physicist at heart,
disguised as a mathematician.

I am a Senior Quantum Research Scientist at LG Electronics Toronto AI Lab. My work sits at the intersection of quantum information, quantum computing, and algorithms, with a particular interest in understanding what makes quantum computation useful and trustworthy.

I was trained as a quantum information theorist in a mathematics environment while earning a PhD in Physics at the University of Calgary. That hybrid background continues to shape how I approach problems: start from structure, then ask what it means operationally.
